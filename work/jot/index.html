01 · The order

What it is.

Jot records a meeting on my Windows laptop, catching both the microphone and the computer's own audio, or on my phone. Whisper turns it into a transcript on the laptop, and then it writes a summary. For each meeting I choose where that summary comes from: Claude, a local model, or Private, where nothing leaves the laptop at all.

Around each note it keeps what a meeting leaves behind: action items in a To do list, the people who were there, a follow-up draft, and attached PDFs, Word files and photos of handwritten notes. Research asks Claude to look something up on the web, and I choose whether to add what it found or discard it.

Jot only ever asks. When another app starts using the microphone it offers to record, but nothing starts or stops without a button press.

02 · Prep

Three modes, three paths.

  • Claude. The laptop transcribes, then my home server asks Claude for the summary and stores the note. The server is the only place the API key lives.
  • Local. A model running on the laptop through Ollama writes the summary, and only the finished note goes to the server.
  • Private. Everything stays in an encrypted vault on the laptop. In this mode Jot only talks to the laptop itself, which is enforced in code and covered by tests.

The phone records and uploads, and the laptop picks its recordings up as jobs, transcribes them and sends the notes back. The phone checks for new notes every 30 seconds while it's open, so a meeting's notes are there soon after it ends.

03 · The cook

The hard parts.

  • A vault built on real cryptography. A random key encrypts every private item with XChaCha20-Poly1305. That key is wrapped twice, once by the master password through Argon2id and once by a recovery key. Search runs in memory after unlock, with no plain-text index on disk.
  • Recording that survives a crash. Every 30 seconds the newest audio is sealed with the vault's public key, so recording keeps going safely even if the vault locks, and anything left over is offered for recovery.
  • Two tracks, never mixed. The mic and the computer's audio go to Whisper separately, so every line knows whether I said it. Mic lines that are only the other people coming through my speakers are dropped.
  • Photos the server can't read. The phone's private camera seals each photo before it leaves the phone and never saves it to the gallery. The server only holds the sealed file until the laptop collects it and reads the handwriting locally.
  • Every transcript treated as hostile. Prompts mark meeting content as data, not instructions, and summaries are shown as sanitized Markdown, never as HTML, with remote images blocked.
  • A buddy that can't leak. A small floating figure shows Jot is listening. It only ever gets one of four moods, never words, and it's hidden from screen shares.
  • Tested at every layer. Hundreds of tests across the Rust core, the server and both apps, with Claude mocked so no test calls the real API.

04 · On the pass

In daily use.

Jot runs on my own laptop, phone and home server. There's no public build, and the source is private.
