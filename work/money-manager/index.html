01 · The order

What it is.

A budget app for a household. It keeps the members of the house, a personal budget for each of them, savings goals, bills, paydays and a purchase log you fill in by hand, and it works out who owes whom.

It's offline-first. Everything is saved on the device you use it on, so it works with no connection and no account. You can look around a sample household before you set up your own, and nothing is saved until you change something.

02 · Prep

Your data, your devices.

It runs as a web app you install from the browser, or as an Android APK. A single phone never needs a server.

Sync is off by default. If two people want the same numbers on two devices, they can carry a backup file across, or point both devices at a database they host themselves. Changes then show up on the other device within seconds, and queue up while a device is offline.

03 · The cook

The hard parts.

  • A sync engine I wrote for it. The newest edit wins, the server clamps device clocks so a phone with the wrong time can't win forever, and a browser harness replays 14 edit-versus-pull races in CI.
  • Dates that survive a time zone. The test suite runs a second time in a Sydney time zone, to catch date bugs before they reach a payday.
  • Money that adds up. Split shares always add back up to the cent: 100.00 split three ways is 33.34, 33.33 and 33.33.
  • Every screen size, checked. A responsive audit drives the app at 11 sizes from 280px to 1920px, and an offline check runs before every deploy.
