Multi-Apocalypse working title

A single-player, third-person survival game set in a small New Zealand town. Zombies come first. Then aliens, fallout and a portal to hell, one at a time, in a different order every new game.

01 · The order

What it is.

The world doesn't end once. It ends slowly, one apocalypse at a time. You play Asher, a chef halfway through retraining as a software developer when the dead get up. Every few days something new arrives, and you can't plan for it, because the order changes every game.

By day you scavenge. By night you hold a safe house: any shop in town can be claimed, boarded up and built out with a workbench, storage and a garage. Along the way you rescue survivors who fight beside you, bring down four motherships, and repair a crashed UFO to fly yourself.

It's built for evenings. Every session has something worth doing, the game saves each time you sleep, and a log tells you what you were in the middle of when you come back.

This is my biggest project, and my third run at the same idea. The first two are in the test kitchen.

The hard parts.

  • An order nobody can plan for. Zombies always come first, and the other three apocalypses are shuffled for every new game, so every system has to hold up whichever one arrives next.
  • A release gate that can say no. One script checks 16 things and prints PASS or a numbered list of what to fix. It blocks a build on placeholder assets, missing licence notices, story spoilers in any text the player can see, and any test that hasn't passed since the scripts last changed.
  • Tests that can't go stale. The play-mode suite runs into the hundreds of files and runs in chunks. A test only counts if its latest result is a pass from a run made after the newest script change.
  • Placeholder until proven otherwise. Every imported model, texture, animation, sound and font counts as a placeholder until it has been reworked. Reworked pieces carry a stamp the release gate can read.
  • Audio from code. Sound effects are synthesised by script and the whole mix is audited against loudness targets. The 358 dialogue lines, about 47 minutes, are generated offline and keyed to subtitles.
  • Nothing phones home. The build tools stay out of the shipped game, and the release can't contact the engine maker's servers.

I build with AI coding agents and hold the result to those tests, that gate and my own review. If a build doesn't pass, it doesn't ship.

04 · On the pass

Not on the pass yet.

Multi-Apocalypse is in production. It runs and plays, the systems above are in, and the art is being reworked piece by piece. There's no public build, and the source is private.

Gameplay clips are on the way. They'll show the systems and the opening of each apocalypse, and nothing of the story.
